Most prompts need nothing more than plain text, perhaps with a [prompt template](/users-guide/image-generation/prompting-guide/#prompt-templates) or a few [dynamic prompt](/users-guide/image-generation/prompting-guide/#dynamic-prompts) alternatives. This page covers the syntax for finer control: making parts of a prompt count for more or less, blending prompts, and the advanced forms of dynamic prompts.

## Attention weighting

Attention weighting makes parts of the prompt count for more or less than the rest. **SD 1.5, SD 2.x and SDXL** use Compel. **Anima** accepts the attention-weight forms shown below and applies the weights to its own text conditioning; it does not run its text encoder through Compel. Other model families, including FLUX, Z-Image, CogView4 and Qwen Image, pass these symbols to the model as ordinary text.

:::note[Compatibility note]
  If a weighted prompt seems to be ignored, check which model you are using. Anima supports the attention-weight forms, but not Compel's `.blend()` or `.and()` functions or embeddings. Its prompt field highlights supported groups and weights. Unsupported method names stay plain, while attention inside their arguments still works. SD 1.5, SD 2.x and SDXL also highlight their supported embeddings and prompt functions.
:::

| Syntax | Effect |
|---|---|
| `trees+` / `fog-` | Increase or decrease a single word |
| `(golden hour light)+` | Weight a phrase |
| `(cinematic lighting)1.25` | Use an exact numeric weight |
| `(portrait with (blue eyes)1.3)1.1` | Nest weights |
| `("portrait photo", "oil painting").blend(0.7, 0.3)` | Blend prompts |
| `("red silk dress", "studio portrait", "soft rim light").and()` | Encode clauses separately |
| `colored pencil \(medium\)` | Use literal parentheses |

### Weighting with `+` and `-`

Append `+` to increase influence, or `-` to reduce it.

```text
freckles+
background crowd-
(soft rim light)++
```

- Single words can be weighted directly. Wrap phrases of several words in parentheses.
- Each additional `+` or `-` compounds, in steps of roughly 10%.
- One or two steps is usually enough. Extreme weighting can overpower the rest of the prompt.

### Numeric weights

Use numeric weights when you want precise control instead of repeated plus or minus markers.

```text
(cinematic lighting)1.25
(background crowd)0.8
```

`1` is neutral, values above `1` increase emphasis, and values between `0` and `1` reduce it. Wrap the weighted phrase in parentheses.

Groups can be nested, and the weights combine. In `(portrait with (blue eyes)1.3)1.1`, the outer group strengthens the whole phrase, and `blue eyes` gets even more emphasis.

### Anima attention weights

Anima supports Compel-style word suffixes (`hair+`, `hair--`), neutral and weighted nested groups (`(long hair)2`, `((hair)+)0.5`), and double-quoted targets (`"red blue"2`, `"red (blue)2"+`). You can also write `hair.attend(2)`, `(long hair).attend(++)`, or `"red blue" .attend (--)`. Spaces are allowed around the explicit `.attend` call; an ordinary numeric or symbolic suffix must directly follow its target.

Nested weights multiply: in `"red (blue)2"+`, red has weight `1.1` and blue has weight `2.2`. Opposite signs also multiply: `(hair-)+` has weight `0.99`. `1` is neutral; `0` clears the affected adapter vectors. Numeric attention is unsigned, and both each local weight and every composed weight must be finite and at most `65504`. Very large weights can destabilize generation. Anima applies weighting after its text encoders rather than using Compel's CLIP conditioning algorithm.

Parentheses group text without an explicit weight. Escape parentheses and double quotes as `\(cosplay\)` and `\"` to keep them literal. Escaped signs such as `hair\+` are neutral. Compel's syntactic escapes `\+`, `\-`, `\.`, `\=`, and `\,` keep their backslash in the encoder text; escaped parentheses and quotes lose it. An unclosed enclosing group such as `((red)2` stays literal, including its apparent inner weight.

Within double quotes, `\n`, `\t` and `\r` decode to whitespace before attention is parsed: `"cat\ndog+"2` weights cat by `2` and dog by `2.2`. The editor and keyboard edits preserve the authored escape spelling. Extra preceding backslashes remain literal, and `\v` stays literal. Mixing `\f` with words makes a quoted target malformed, so it stays neutral literal source. Outside quotes, these backslash-letter sequences stay literal.

Fragment boundaries follow Compel's context-sensitive attention grammar. At top level, `a(b)2` and `foo.bar+` stay literal; inside `(a(b)2)` or `(foo.bar+)2`, the inner attention is active. Parsed adjacent fragments are separated by a space when no authored ASCII whitespace separates them: `red+(blue)2` becomes `red blue`, `(red)2_blue` becomes `red _blue`, and `(foo.bar+)2` becomes `foo . bar`. Commas also separate parsed fragments: `red,blue+` becomes `red, blue`, while `(red)2,blue` becomes `red , blue`. Authored whitespace, including newlines, is retained rather than normalized. Prompts without parsed attention, grouping, or decoded escapes keep their exact text. Nonbreaking spaces, em spaces and U+0085 belong to the same bare fragment rather than creating an ASCII word boundary.

Anima does not execute `.and()`, `.blend()`, `.swap()` or embeddings. Method punctuation stays literal, but supported attention in their arguments remains active, including incomplete calls while typing: `.and(dog+)` still weights `dog`. A method's leading dot is retained after a numeric suffix, so `(cat)2.and(dog+)` weights cat by `2` and dog by `1.1` without executing `.and()`.

The Anima model card uses ComfyUI's `(chibi:2)` form. In InvokeAI write `(chibi)2`: `(chibi:2)` is neutral grouping and sends `chibi:2` to the encoder.

### Adjusting weights with the keyboard

In a prompt field, select text and press **Ctrl+Up** or **Ctrl+Down** to raise or lower its attention. With no selection, the shortcut targets the word at the cursor. Selecting a group's parentheses or weight targets its contents. The shortcut works with SD 1.5, SD 2.x, SDXL and Anima; with other models the keys keep their usual text-editing behavior. For Anima, punctuation within a Compel-style word such as `blue-green` or `hair!` remains part of that word.

- A selection inside a group changes only the selected words. A selection crossing a group boundary changes the selected words on both sides.
- Symbolic weights move one `+` or `-` step. Numeric weights change by `0.1` of effective attention, including inherited group weights.
- The numeric attention preference controls new weights; existing group weights keep their style.
- For SD models, adjacent content with matching weights merges. Anima edits preserve unselected source punctuation, escapes, method calls and effective weights. The adjusted content stays selected for the next keypress.
- Selecting an Anima group delimiter changes the whole group. With differing numeric descendant weights, each selected effective weight changes by `0.1`. Whole zero-weight groups can increase to `0.1`; a partial edit under zero stays unchanged when the other weights cannot be preserved. An edit that would change literal text, produce a negative numeric weight or exceed the weight bounds also stays unchanged.
- For SD prompt functions, the shortcut adjusts prompt arguments and preserves function parameters. For Anima, supported attention in literal method arguments can be adjusted too. Use **Ctrl+Z** to undo a weight change.

### Blending with `.blend()`

Use `.blend()` to mix the meaning of two or more prompts.

```text
("portrait photo, 85mm lens", "oil painting, visible brushstrokes").blend(0.7, 0.3)
```

Provide one weight for each prompt. Keeping the weights near a total of `1` makes the result easier to reason about, and quoting each prompt is safest, especially when they contain commas.

### Combining clauses with `.and()`

Use `.and()` when you want separate prompt clauses encoded individually instead of as one long comma-separated sentence.

```text
("red silk dress", "studio portrait", "soft rim light").and()
```

This can behave differently from `red silk dress, studio portrait, soft rim light`. If a normal prompt keeps collapsing ideas together, `.and()` is worth testing.

### Literal parentheses

Unescaped parentheses are treated as weighting syntax. To include actual parentheses in the text, escape them with backslashes:

```text
colored pencil \(medium\)
A bear \(with razor-sharp teeth\) in a forest
```

## Advanced dynamic prompt syntax

These additions to the [dynamic prompt](/users-guide/image-generation/prompting-guide/#dynamic-prompts) syntax give finer control over how variations are built.

### Choosing more than one option

Put a number and `$$` at the start of a group to pick several different options from it. They are joined with a comma:

```text
portrait, {2$$rim light|fog|rain}
```

gives prompts such as `portrait, rim light,fog` and `portrait, fog,rain`. To join them differently, put the separator between two `$$`:

```text
portrait, {2$$ and $$rim light|fog|rain}
```

gives prompts such as `portrait, rim light and fog`. A range picks a varying number of options: `{1-3$$rim light|fog|rain}` picks one, two or three.

### Weighting options

In **Random sample** mode, you can make some options more likely than others by putting a weight and `::` in front of them:

```text
a {0.7::red|0.2::green|0.1::blue} balloon
```

makes red balloons most common. Weights are ignored in All combinations mode, where every option appears once.

### Variables

A variable gives a group a name, so you can define it once and use it in several places. `${name={...}}` defines it and `${name}` uses it:

```text
${color={red|blue}} a ${color} hat with a ${color} scarf
```

Each use varies on its own, like a group written out in full, so this gives all four combinations from `a red hat with a red scarf` to `a blue hat with a blue scarf`. Variables are most useful for long lists you would otherwise repeat; for lists you use in many prompts, a [wildcard](/users-guide/image-generation/prompting-guide/#wildcards) is more convenient.

### Comments

Everything after a `#` is treated as a comment and left out of the prompt. Avoid `#` in ordinary prompts, since the rest of the prompt after it is dropped.
